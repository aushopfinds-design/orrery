## Required reading experience

Every substantive chapter follows this order:
1. An ordinary, specific situation the reader can recognise.
2. What the person might do or feel, including the useful side and the difficulty.
3. A short explanation linking that interpretation to the actual calculated Saju feature.
4. Two workable responses, with the circumstances that make each useful.
5. A chapter-specific way to notice whether the response helped.

Write connected paragraphs, not five compulsory labels or a coaching worksheet. Use headings only where they improve reading. Situations are possibilities, not invented facts about the customer. Prefer “If this happens…” or “You might recognise…” over claiming to know the reader’s job, partner, history or feelings.

A chart interpretation must remain an interpretation. Do not imply that a birth chart proves an observed behaviour, diagnoses a condition or guarantees an event.

## Problems identified in the current source

Inspected complete-reading.ts, category-readings.ts and decision-focus.ts.

- The opening synthesis in decision-focus.ts starts with “Your birth month brings…” and named themes before the reader meets a concrete situation.
- Phrases such as “decision rights”, “principal inner-stem relationship”, “acceptance standard” and “editorial theme families” describe internal machinery or management concepts.
- “Choose one…”, “small attempt”, “review” and “clear agreement” appear across subjects. Changing the noun is not enough to make the chapters distinct.
- The current review sentence appends a general personal need to many subjects; it can sound assembled rather than written for that chapter.
- Technical evidence is already available separately. Keep that traceability, but explain the meaning in everyday English inside the story.

| activate your potential | name the actual skill or behaviour being discussed |
| principal inner-stem relationship | the exact technical term stays in the optional chart notes |

Keep necessary chart words, but explain each at first use. For example: “The birth-day stem is the part of the chart this reading uses as its starting point.” Do not describe one position as the whole personality.

## Evidence contract for implementation

Each passage needs an internal evidence record: chapter ID, calculated feature, exact value, mapping rule and the interpretation selected. The visible explanation should name the relevant position in plain English and explain why it led to this passage.

The drafts below are conditional copy models. They are NOT readings calculated for the owner or any customer. The bracketed evidence instruction must be resolved from the actual chart before publication. Do not show placeholders to customers. Do not attach a responsibility, expression or learning passage to a chart merely because it reads well.

Only describe strength, useful elements, structural patterns, favourable periods or interactions when the calculation and interpretation actually support that claim. Repeated symbols alone do not establish strength. Unknown birth time must not silently select an hour-based interpretation.

## 1. Complete Life Reading — when helping becomes your default

You might recognise this moment: someone asks for a favour, and you say yes before checking what you already have to do. Later, you are still finishing their task while your own plans wait.

Being dependable can make people feel safe around you. The difficult part comes when they stop asking whether you have time, and you stop asking yourself.

[Evidence instruction: use this passage only when the selected, calculated responsibility interpretation supports it. Name the actual birth-month feature and explain its connection to the birth-day stem. Do not claim a strong responsibility pattern without a strength assessment.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “This reading compares the symbol in your birth month with your birth-day stem. It connects that relationship with duty and expectations, which is why this section looks at how easily a request can become your responsibility.”

If you want to help, offer the part you can actually finish: “I can do the first half tonight.” If your week is already full, give yourself time before answering: “Let me check and get back to you.” Notice whether your own plans still have a place after you have helped.

Chapter purpose: recognising automatic responsibility. Do not reuse this same favour-setting scene in the career or wellbeing chapter.

## 2. Love Compatibility — advice when someone wanted understanding

Imagine one of you comes home upset. The other starts suggesting solutions. Within minutes, both feel misunderstood: one wanted to be heard, while the other thought they were helping.

Offering an answer can be an act of care. So can listening without trying to fix anything. Trouble starts when each person expects the other to recognise their version of care without being told.

[Evidence instruction: select this scene only for a supported difference in the two calculated relationship profiles. Explain Person A’s and Person B’s actual selected features separately. If the profiles do not differ in this way, use a different scene.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “The relationship passages selected for your two birth-day symbols describe different ways of responding when something feels wrong. One leans towards offering an answer; the other gives more weight to feeling understood first. This is the difference being explored here, rather than a compatibility score.”

Try asking, “Do you want me to listen, or help you think through it?” If neither person can talk calmly, agree when to return to it. Look for a change in how the conversation ends: does each person feel understood, even when the problem is still there?

Chapter purpose: a mismatch in the way care is offered. Other relationship chapters must use different moments, such as planning time together or repairing a disagreement.

## 3. Career — a new task without a clear priority

You are nearly finished with one task when your manager gives you another. Both sound urgent. You keep switching between them, and by the end of the day neither feels properly done.

You may be trying to show that you can be trusted. But accepting both tasks does not tell you which one matters most. Without that answer, working harder may only leave you more frustrated.

[Evidence instruction: use the actual work interpretation selected from the birth-month feature and its relation to the birth-day stem. If the chart selects a learning or creative-work route, use a matching situation instead of this duty-based example.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “This part of your reading uses the relationship between your birth-month symbol and your birth-day stem. Here it is read through work expectations: how you respond when someone depends on you to deliver.”

You could ask, “If I start this now, should I move the other deadline?” If the first deadline cannot move, explain what can realistically be finished today. The useful sign is a clearer order of work, rather than a longer evening at your desk.

Chapter purpose: negotiating competing work priorities. Keep it separate from business pricing and general personal boundaries.

## 4. Business — the sale that takes longer than expected

A customer accepts your price. Then come the extra messages, another revision and a small change that turns into an evening of work. You made the sale, but the money no longer feels worth the time.

Wanting to give good service can help people trust you. The difficulty is deciding where the original purchase ends and a new request begins.

[Evidence instruction: choose this passage only for the calculated business route concerned with the effort and cost behind a result. Preserve the actual feature value in the evidence record; do not imply the chart predicts business success.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “The business passage selected from your birth-month relationship focuses on what a result takes to deliver. That is why this section looks beyond the price to the work included in it.”

For the next order, list what the price covers and how many changes are included. If customers often need the extra help, offer it as a separate option rather than quietly adding it to every sale. Track the time spent on one complete order, including messages after delivery. That gives you something concrete to use when setting the next price.

Chapter purpose: the cost of service and scope changes. Do not turn this into investment advice or reuse a career-manager scene.

## 5. Everyday Balance — the job you notice becomes the job you do

You sit down for a break, spot something that needs doing and get up again. It is a small task, but there always seems to be another one after it.

Being quick to notice what is needed can make daily life run smoothly. It can also make rest difficult when noticing a task feels the same as agreeing to do it.

[Evidence instruction: use only if the selected everyday interpretation concerns taking responsibility for unfinished tasks. This is ordinary-life reflection, not a health diagnosis or a claim that the chart explains fatigue.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “The responsibility passage selected for your birth-month relationship looks at how quickly an expectation can become something you feel you should handle. Here that idea is applied to everyday tasks.”

If the task can wait, choose a time to do it after your break. If someone else is responsible, let them know it is there rather than automatically taking over. Notice whether a break has a real ending you chose, instead of being interrupted by the next thing you see.

Chapter purpose: switching out of task mode. No medical advice, diagnosis or generic recommendation to “find balance”.

## 6. Year Ahead — an idea that never reaches the first attempt

You start the year wanting to learn something new. Soon you have saved several courses and made a detailed plan, but you have not tried the thing itself.

Preparation may help you feel ready. It becomes less useful when every new source gives you another reason to postpone the first attempt.

[Evidence instruction: this model applies only when the actual selected annual relationship maps to the learning interpretation. Name the selected year and the annual feature, explain how it relates to the birth-day stem, and distinguish it from the birth-month feature. Do not invent a favourable month.]

Possible evidence wording after that check: “For [selected year], this reading compares the year’s stem with your birth-day stem. It selects a learning theme, so the question here is how learning could turn into something you actually use.”

Before buying another course, try the simplest version of the skill with what you already know. If you get stuck at a specific step, look for help with that step. Keep the result of the attempt, however rough it is. It shows what you can now do and what still needs practice.

Chapter purpose: applying the particular annual interpretation. Every month and ten-year period must use its own calculated evidence and a different decision; do not repeat this lesson with a changed date.

## 7. Complete Bundle — a joined-up reading, not three copies of the same advice

The bundle is not a seventh chart interpretation. It combines Complete, Compatibility and Year Ahead. Give each included report its own purpose and avoid presenting the same advice three times.

Draft navigation copy:

“You might find it easiest to start with the part closest to what is happening in your life. The Complete Life Reading looks at patterns in your own choices. Love Compatibility explores what happens between you and the second person. Year Ahead adds the selected year’s interpretation to your personal reading.

If work is taking up most of your attention, begin with the work chapter in your Complete Life Reading. If a conversation keeps going wrong, start with Compatibility. If you are weighing plans for the selected year, start with Year Ahead. You do not need to turn every chapter into a task.”

Use the actual selected year in the final copy. Keep Person A as the subject of Complete and Year Ahead, and both people in Compatibility. Cross-reference related ideas briefly rather than copying an entire paragraph. Already-issued paid report snapshots remain immutable.

## Chapter distinction and repetition review

| Area | Owns this question | Must not become another copy of |
|---|---|---|
| Personal patterns | What do I tend to do before I notice? | Workload negotiation |
| Career | What work arrangement lets me do the job? | Customer pricing |
| Money | What spending or sharing decision is being discussed? | Generic “value your time” advice |
| Business | What does the customer receive, and what does delivery cost? | Career progression |
| Relationships | What happens between these two people in this moment? | Solo boundary advice |
| Everyday balance | What happens when the task is meant to be over? | Medical or productivity advice |
| Year and periods | What changes in the calculated reference and the decision explored? | The same lesson with a new date |

Review repeated meaning, not only identical sentences. For each chapter, record its situation, selected feature, central tension, proposed choice and success sign. If two chapters have the same situation and action after removing their titles, rewrite one. Repeated evidence may be referenced, but must lead to an honestly distinct application rather than invented extra personal traits.

Do not force every chart into seven different personalities. When the same feature matters in more than one chapter, explain the connection and apply it to a genuinely different decision.
